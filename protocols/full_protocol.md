1.	Cellulase Activity Assay
Cellulase activity is typically measured by quantifying the reducing sugars released from cellulosic substrates using the 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid method (Chantarasiri, 2020; Ghose, 1987).
Principle: Cellulase enzymes hydrolyse cellulose, releasing reducing sugars (e.g., glucose). These reducing sugars react with 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid under alkaline and hot conditions to form 3-amino-5-nitrosalicylic acid, a reddish-brown compound, which can be quantified spectrophotometrically.
Parameters	Details
Enzyme Production	•	Production medium (per litre): CMC (20 g), peptone (20 g), NaCl (1 g), CaCl₂ (0.005 g), MgSO₄ (0.82 g), K₂HPO₄ (1.25 g), KH₂PO₄ (3 g), FeSO₄ (0.01 g), ZnSO₄ (0.005 g), MnCl₂ (0.0001 g), NH₄Cl (1 g) (Lisdiyanti et al., 2012).
•	Incubate at 37°C for 36 hours with continuous shaking at 150 rpm. 
•	Centrifuge at 10,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C to obtain cell-free enzyme extract.
Substrate	1% Carboxymethylcellulase (CMC)
Assay procedure
(Zhang et al., 2010)	•	Reaction mixture: 1 ml of 1% CMC substrate and 1 ml of enzyme extract.
•	Incubate at 55°C for 15 minutes.
•	Terminate the reaction by adding 2 ml of DNS* reagent, followed by boiling at 95°C for 5 minutes. 
•	Measure the absorbance at 540 nm using a spectrophotometer.
*Composition: 10.6 g 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid, 19.8 g NaOH, 306 g sodium potassium tartrate, 7.6 mL molten phenol, 8.3 g sodium metabisulfite in 1416 mL distilled water
2.	Hemicellulase Activity Assay
Similar to cellulase, hemicellulase (e.g., xylanase) activity can be quantified using the DNS method by measuring released reducing sugars (Ghose and Bisaria, 1987).
Principle: Hemicellulase enzymes hydrolyze hemicellulose (e.g., xylan), releasing reducing sugars (e.g., xylose). The DNS reagent reacts with the free reducing sugar ends under alkaline and high-temperature conditions to produce a coloured compound.
Parameters	Details
Enzyme Production	•	Production medium (per litre): peptone (10 g), KH₂PO₄ (1.5 g), NaNO₃ (2 g), NaCl (0.5 g), MgSO₄ (0.5 g), CaCl₂ (0.25 g), FeSO₄ (0.001 g), ZnSO₄ (0.001 g), CuSO₄ (0.001 g), birchwood xylan (10 g).
•	Incubate at 30°C with continuous shaking at 150 rpm for optimal enzyme production. 
•	Centrifuge at 10,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C to obtain cell-free enzyme extract.
Substrate	1% (w/v) oat spelt xylan
Assay procedure Miller (1959) and Saha (2002)	•	Reaction mixture: 0.5 ml of 1% (w/v) oat spelt xylan dissolved in 50 mM acetate buffer (pH 5.0) and 0.5 ml of appropriately diluted culture supernatant 
•	Incubate at 50°C for 30 minutes.
•	Terminate the reaction by adding equal volume of DNS reagent, followed by boiling at 95°C for 5 minutes. 
•	Measure the absorbance at 540 nm using a spectrophotometer.

3.	Pectinase Activity Assay
Pectinase activity is determined by quantifying the release of reducing sugars (e.g., galacturonic acid) from pectin using the DNS method (Merin et al., 2011; Mohandas et al., 2018).
Principle: Pectinase enzymes break down pectin, yielding reducing sugars that react with DNS reagent to form a measurable coloured product.
Parameters	Details
Enzyme Production	•	Production medium (per litre): KH₂PO₄ (3g), Na₂HPO₄ (6g), NH₄Cl (2g), NaCl (5g), MgSO₄·7H₂O (0.1g), pectin (2g), (Chatterjee et al., 2016)
•	Incubate at 37°C with continuous shaking at 150 rpm for optimal enzyme production for 48 hours. 
•	Centrifuge at 8,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 4°C to obtain cell-free enzyme extract.
Substrate	0.5% (w/v) citrus pectin in 0.1M phosphate buffer (pH 7.5)
Assay procedure	•	Reaction mixture: 900 μl of pectin substrate with 100 μl of crude enzyme extract.
•	Incubate at 50°C for 10 minutes.
•	Terminate the reaction by adding 2 ml of DNS reagent, followed by boiling at 92°C for 10 minutes. 
•	Measure the absorbance at 540 nm using a spectrophotometer.

4.	Lipase Activity Assay
Principle: The para-nitrophenyl butyrate (PNPB) colorimetric method quantifies lipase activity by measuring the amount of para-nitrophenol (pNP) released from the enzymatic hydrolysis of PNPB. Lipase cleaves the ester bond of the colourless PNPB substrate, yielding pNP and butyric acid. Under alkaline conditions, pNP is yellow and can be detected spectrophotometrically at around 405 nm. The intensity of the yellow colour is directly proportional to the amount of pNP released, which in turn correlates with the lipase activity (Gupta et al., 2002).
Parameters	Details
Enzyme Production	•	Production medium (per litre): glucose (20g), yeast extract (10g), peptone (10g), MgSO₄ (0.09g), MnSO₄ (0.03g), CuSO₄·5H₂O (1.5mg), KCl (0.5g) and olive oil (5ml) adjusted to pH 7.2 (Patel et al., 2016).
•	Incubate at 37°C for 72 hours with gentle agitation.
•	Centrifuge at 10,000 rpm for 20 minutes at 4°C to obtain cell-free enzyme extract.
Substrate	100 μL of the prepared 27 mM PNPB** solution 
Assay procedure (Zibaee et al., 2012)	•	Reaction mixture: 30 μl of crude enzyme extract and 100 μl of PNPB substrate (27 mM)
•	Incubate at 37°C for 10 minutes.
•	Terminate the reaction by adding 100 μl of distilled water and immediately measure the absorbance at 405 nm using a spectrophotometer.
**dissolve the required amount of PNPB (≈57 mg for 10 mL) in a small volume of isopropanol. Slowly add this stock to the desired aqueous buffer (e.g., 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7–8 or phosphate buffer pH 7.6) with continuous stirring.
5.	Invertase Activity Assay
Invertase activity can be quantified by measuring the reducing sugars (glucose and fructose) released from sucrose hydrolysis using the DNS method (Lase et al., 2021).
Principle: Invertase hydrolyses sucrose into glucose and fructose. These reducing sugars react with DNS reagent to produce a coloured complex, which can be measured spectrophotometrically.
Parameters	Details
Enzyme Production	•	Production medium (per litre): Sucrose 2%, peptone 0.5%, yeast extract 0.5%, KH₂PO₄ 0.15%, Na₂HPO₄ 0.2%, MgSO₄ 0.05%, CaCl₂ 0.01%
•	Incubate at 37°C for 24 hours.
•	Centrifuge at 10,000 rpm for 30 minutes at 4°C to obtain cell-free enzyme extract.
Substrate	1% (w/v) sucrose solution in distilled water.
Assay procedure (Sumner and Howell, 1935)	•	Reaction mixture: 0.5 ml of crude enzyme extract, 0.5 ml of 0.01M sodium acetate buffer (pH 4.5) and 1 ml of 1% (w/v) sucrose substrate solution.
•	Incubate at 100°C for 15 minutes.
•	Terminate the reaction by adding 2 ml of DNS reagent, followed by boiling at 92°C for 10 minutes. 
•	Measure the absorbance at 540 nm using a spectrophotometer.

6.	Protein Estimation
The Bradford assay is a rapid and sensitive method for protein quantification, relying on the shift in absorbance of Coomassie Brilliant Blue G-250 dye when it binds to proteins. Bovine Serum Albumin is commonly used as a standard (Bradford, 1976).
Principle: The Coomassie Brilliant Blue G-250 dye binds primarily to basic and aromatic amino acid residues in proteins, causing a shift in the dye's absorption maximum from 465 nm (red) to 595 nm (blue). The intensity of the blue colour is proportional to the amount of protein present.
Parameters	Details
Standards and Sample Preparation	•	Bovine Serum Albumin (BSA) stock solution at 1-2 mg/mL in distilled water. 
•	Prepare standard series: 5-50 µg/mL BSA by dilution in deionized water. Use 100 µL of BSA standard or unknown sample (diluted to 5-50 µg/mL protein concentration).
Assay procedure (Bradford, 1976)	•	Add 100 µL sample/standard to microplate well or cuvette.
•	Add 100 µL of Bradford reagent (diluted 2.5-fold in distilled water). Total reaction volume: ~200 µL (microplate) or 1 mL (cuvette). 
•	Incubate at room temperature in the dark for at least 5 minutes. 
•	Measure absorbance at 595 nm using spectrophotometer or microplate reader.

•	Measure absorbance at 540 nm and record the absorbance values
